## Summary

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued circular RBI/2026-27/293 (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 27) dated October 12, 2026, introducing significant regulatory changes regarding risk management and inter-bank dealings. Specifically, non-deliverable derivative contracts (NDDCs) involving INR offered to non-resident users are now restricted exclusively to hedging purposes, and a Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) has been mandated for specific foreign exchange derivative contracts.

## Key Points

- **NDDC Restrictions:** Authorised Dealer Category-I banks with IFSC Banking Units and Standalone Primary Dealers (AD Cat-III) may now only offer INR-involving non-deliverable derivative contracts (NDDCs) to non-resident users for hedging purposes, removing prior purpose-free flexibility.
- **Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR):** ADs must maintain an FERR for foreign exchange derivative contracts involving INR undertaken to hedge currency risk of their own assets and liabilities where the AD is purchasing foreign currency against INR.
- **FERR Calculation:** The FERR must be equal to 20 percent of the INR equivalent of the notional amount of each derivative contract.
- **Deposit & Maintenance:** FERR must be deposited and maintained in cash in India with the Reserve Bank on a daily basis until contract termination.

## Regulatory Changes

- **Purpose Limitation on NDDCs:** Reverses previous relaxations permitting non-resident users to enter into INR NDDCs without purpose restrictions. Hedging is now mandatory.
- **Mandatory Reserve Requirement:** Introduction of a 20% cash-backed Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve deposited daily with the RBI for proprietary asset/liability hedging involving foreign currency purchases against INR.

## Compliance Requirements

- Authorised Dealers must immediately update their risk management systems and compliance frameworks to restrict INR NDDCs to non-residents strictly for hedging.
- ADs must calculate, deposit, and maintain daily cash reserves equivalent to 20% of the notional amount of qualifying foreign exchange derivative contracts with the RBI.

## Important Dates

- **Issuance Date:** October 12, 2026.
- **Effective Date:** Applicable to foreign exchange derivative contracts undertaken after the issuance of these Directions.

## Impact Assessment

- Increased capital and liquidity constraints for Authorised Dealers engaging in INR derivative contracts.
- Elimination of speculative or non-hedging INR NDDC transactions by non-residents through Indian IFSC Banking Units and Standalone Primary Dealers, tightening offshore/onsite derivative flows.
