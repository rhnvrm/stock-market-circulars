## Summary

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued Circular RBI/2026-27/292 (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 26) dated October 10, 2026, introducing the Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) for foreign exchange derivative contracts involving INR. Authorised Dealers are now required to maintain a 20% cash reserve with RBI for eligible derivative contracts.

## Key Points

- **Requirement of FERR**: Authorised Dealers must maintain a Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve for INR foreign exchange derivative contracts with users.
- **Applicability**: Applies to derivative contracts involving INR with a notional value exceeding USD 2 million equivalent, undertaken for hedging current account transactions where the user purchases foreign currency against INR.
- **Reserve Amount**: The reserve must be equal to 20% of the INR equivalent of the notional amount of each contract.
- **Maintenance**: Deposited and maintained by way of cash in India with the RBI on a daily basis until contract termination.
- **Anti-Circumvention**: Splitting transactions across one or more Authorised Dealers to bypass the USD 2 million threshold is prohibited and treated as a FEMA violation.
- **Reporting**: Details of the FERR maintained must be reported daily through RBI's Centralised Information Management System (CIMS).

## Regulatory Changes

- Mandates a daily cash deposit of 20% of the notional amount for qualifying INR forex derivative contracts.
- Introduces strict monitoring and reporting via CIMS to prevent circumvention of hedging regulations.

## Compliance Requirements

- Authorised Dealers must calculate, deposit, and daily maintain 20% cash reserves for applicable derivative contracts.
- Users must ensure that splitting transactions to avoid the reserve threshold is strictly avoided, as it constitutes regulatory non-compliance.
- Authorised Dealers must report FERR details daily via CIMS.

## Important Dates

- **Issuance Date**: October 10, 2026.
- **Effective Date**: Applicable to foreign exchange derivative contracts undertaken after the issuance of these directions.

## Impact Assessment

- Increases capital and cash holding requirements for large corporate hedging transactions involving INR.
- May raise hedging costs for importers and corporate entities purchasing foreign currency for current account transactions exceeding USD 2 million.
