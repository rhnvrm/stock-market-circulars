## Summary

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) released the minutes of the 63rd meeting of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), held between October 5 and 7, 2026. The MPC voted unanimously to increase the policy repo rate under the liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) by 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent. Consequently, the standing deposit facility (SDF) rate was adjusted to 5.25 per cent, and the marginal standing facility (MSF) rate and Bank Rate to 5.75 per cent. Furthermore, the MPC voted to change its monetary policy stance to "calibrated tightening," indicating that rate cuts are off the table in the near term and future actions will be limited to rate hikes or pauses based on evolving macroeconomic conditions.

## Key Points

- **Repo Rate Hike:** Policy repo rate increased by 25 bps to 5.50%; SDF adjusted to 5.25%; MSF and Bank Rate adjusted to 5.75%.
- **Policy Stance:** Stance shifted from neutral to "calibrated tightening."
- **Growth Outlook:** Real GDP growth for 2026-27 projected at 7.1%, supported by resilient domestic demand, robust services exports, and strong fixed investment.
- **Inflation Outlook:** CPI inflation for 2026-27 projected at 5.2%, with Q3 expected to hit 6.0% due to supply-side pressures, deficient monsoon, El Niño conditions, and higher energy/commodity prices.
- **Dissent/Differing Views:** Dr. Nagesh Kumar and Prof. Ram Singh favoured retaining a neutral stance, though all members voted in favour of the 25 bps rate hike.

## Regulatory Changes

- Policy repo rate raised to 5.50 per cent under the liquidity adjustment facility (LAF).
- Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) rate set at 5.25 per cent.
- Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rate and Bank Rate set at 5.75 per cent.
- Monetary policy stance formally revised to calibrated tightening.

## Compliance Requirements

- Banks and regulated financial entities must adjust lending and deposit benchmark rates (such as Repo-Linked Lending Rates - RLLR) in response to the 25 bps policy rate hike.
- Liquidity management and asset-liability management (ALM) strategies must account for tightened domestic financial conditions and calibrated tightening stance.

## Important Dates

- **Meeting Dates:** October 5 to 7, 2026
- **Publication Date:** October 21, 2026
- **Next MPC Meeting:** December 2 to 4, 2026

## Impact Assessment

- **Borrowing Costs:** Higher benchmark interest rates will lead to increased borrowing costs for retail, corporate, and MSME borrowers, potentially moderating credit growth in interest-rate sensitive sectors.
- **Market Liquidity:** Calibrated tightening signals tighter liquidity conditions and anchors inflation expectations against supply-side shocks and rising input costs.
- **Banking Sector:** Deposit and lending rates across commercial banks are expected to re-price upward, impacting net interest margins (NIMs) and credit demand.
