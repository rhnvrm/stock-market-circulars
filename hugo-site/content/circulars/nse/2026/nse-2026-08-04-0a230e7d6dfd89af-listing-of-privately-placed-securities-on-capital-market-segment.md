## Summary

The National Stock Exchange of India Limited (NSE) has announced the listing of privately placed debt securities (non-convertible debentures) on its capital market segment. Pursuant to Regulation 3.1.1 and Regulation 2.5.5 of the National Stock Exchange (Capital Market) Trading Regulations Part A, these securities are admitted to dealings with effect from August 05, 2026, with the designated security codes, symbols, series, and market lot sizes as specified in the Annexure.

## Key Points

- **Regulatory Reference**: The listing and market lot designations are issued pursuant to Regulations 3.1.1 and 2.5.5 of the National Stock Exchange (Capital Market) Trading Regulations Part A.
- **Effective Date**: The listings and designated lot sizes will take effect on August 05, 2026.
- **Market Lot Size**: The designated lot size for all the listed debt securities is set at 1.

## Regulatory Changes

There are no new regulatory policy changes introduced by this circular. It is an operational enactment under the existing frameworks of Regulation 3.1.1 (Admission to Dealings) and Regulation 2.5.5 (Lot Sizes) of the National Stock Exchange (Capital Market) Trading Regulations Part A.

## Compliance Requirements

- **Trading Codes**: Members must ensure that the securities are identified only by their designated codes and symbols for trading on the NSE systems.
- **Lot Size Requirements**: Trading in these securities must be carried out in the specified market lot size of 1.
- **Database Updates**: Trading members, brokers, and clearing institutions should update their back-office systems and security master databases to include these new listings with their correct ISINs, symbols, and maturity schedules before trading commences on August 05, 2026.

## Impact Assessment

- **Market Impact**: Low. The listing of these privately placed NCDs offers incremental fixed-income investment options for institutions and high-net-worth individuals (HNIs), but has no broader impact on secondary equity market indexes or trading volumes.
- **Operational Impact**: Low. Confined to stock brokers, market participants, and depositories who trade and clear these specific debt instruments, requiring them to register the new symbols/ISINs in their systems.
- **Corporate Impact**: Positive. The issuers benefit from the admission of their debt securities to exchange trading, enhancing liquidity, price discovery, and visibility of their instruments among secondary market investors.
