## Summary

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) issued a final order (Ref: QJA/MN/CFID/CFID-SEC4/32566/2026-27) on July 31, 2026, in the matter of unauthorized pledging of immovable property of Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. (ZEEL). The investigation revealed that ZEEL's prime immovable property in Hyderabad (Jubilee Hills) was pledged as collateral security for ₹726 crores of loans taken by four Essel Group entities from Indiabulls Housing Finance Limited (IHFL) without the knowledge, approval, or consent of the ZEEL Board, Audit Committee, or shareholders. SEBI found that the ultimate ownership and control of these borrowing entities belonged to the promoters of ZEEL, including Subhash Chandra and Punit Goenka. Consequently, SEBI has imposed market bans and substantial monetary penalties on ZEEL, Subhash Chandra, and Punit Goenka.

## Key Points

- **The Infraction:** ZEEL’s Hyderabad land (admeasuring 17,639.64 sq. meters) was unauthorizedly used as security for ₹726 crores in loans availed by four Essel Group entities (Gnex Projects, Vivek Infracon, Gnex Infrabuild, and Renu Realtech) from IHFL on December 13, 2016.
- **Diversion and Control:** The borrowing entities and co-borrower Essel Home were ultimately traced back to the control of Subhash Chandra, Punit Goenka, and their family members via multiple corporate layers and trusts (Celestine Family Trust).
- **Missing Title Deeds:** The issue came to light during SEBI's investigation following the statutory auditors' report for FY 2018-19 noting that the title deeds for the Hyderabad land were missing.
- **Deceptive Scheme:** SEBI concluded that the Noticees engaged in a deceptive device/scheme to divert the listed company's assets to fund promoter-linked privately-held entities, violating PFUTP and SEBI LODR Regulations.
- **Market Ban Imposed:**
  - **Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. (ZEEL):** Barred from the securities market for **2 months**.
  - **Subhash Chandra & Punit Goenka:** Barred from the securities market and prohibited from holding any directorship or Key Managerial Position (KMP) in any listed company/intermediary for **12 months**.
- **Financial Penalties:**
  - **Subhash Chandra:** Fined ₹60 lakh.
  - **Punit Goenka:** Fined ₹58 lakh.
  - **ZEEL:** Fined ₹30 lakh.

## Regulatory Changes

- This is an enforcement action under Sections 11(1), 11(4), 11(4A), 11B(1), and 11B(2) of the SEBI Act, 1992, read with Rule 5 of the SEBI (Procedure for Holding Inquiry and Imposing Penalties) Rules, 1995.
- Demonstrates strict regulatory enforcement against related party transactions, unauthorized asset pledging, and non-disclosure violations under the SEBI LODR and SEBI PFUTP Regulations.

## Compliance Requirements

- **Directorship and Key Positions:** Subhash Chandra and Punit Goenka must immediately step down and refrain from holding any Key Managerial Personnel (KMP) or directorial positions in any listed company or SEBI-registered intermediary for the 12-month period.
- **Payment of Penalties:** Fines must be deposited with SEBI within the stipulated timeline outlined in the order.
- **Internal Governance and Control:** Listed entities must maintain rigid asset controls and audit tracking, ensuring all pledge transactions are duly approved by the Audit Committee, Board of Directors, and disclosed transparently in financial reports.

## Important Dates

- **Order Date:** July 31, 2026.
- **Effective Date:** Immediate effect upon issuance of the order.
- **Market Ban Duration:**
  - ZEEL: 2 months from the order date.
  - Subhash Chandra and Punit Goenka: 12 months from the order date.

## Impact Assessment

- **Governance and Management Impact:** Extreme impact on ZEEL's leadership structure due to the 12-month ban on Punit Goenka from holding key positions, requiring rapid executive succession planning.
- **Market & Operational Impact:** The 2-month market ban on ZEEL itself introduces operational and financing constraints, potentially restricting public fundraising or secondary market activities during this window.
- **Reputational Risk:** Severe damage to investor confidence, given the explicit findings of promoters diverting listed assets to benefit family-controlled entities.
- **Legal Recourse:** The Noticees have appealed to the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) challenging the order, which remains the primary avenue for potential interim relief.
