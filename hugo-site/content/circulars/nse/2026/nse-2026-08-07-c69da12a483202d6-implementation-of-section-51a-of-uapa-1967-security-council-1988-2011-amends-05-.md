## Summary

The National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) has issued a circular regarding the implementation of Section 51A of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 (UAPA). This follows a communication from SEBI concerning amendments made by the United Nations Security Council (UNSC) Committee established pursuant to resolution 1988 (2011), which updated five entries in its Taliban Sanctions List. Stock exchanges, depositories, KYC Registration Agencies (KRAs), and registered intermediaries are advised to take note of these amendments to ensure immediate compliance.

## Key Points

- **UNSC List Amendments**: The UNSC Committee established pursuant to resolution 1988 (2011) has amended five entries in the Taliban Sanctions List, which are subject to assets freeze, travel ban, and arms embargo under Security Council resolution 2816 (2026).
- **UAPA Compliance**: Clause 54 of the SEBI Master Circular on AML/CFT Standards (dated June 06, 2024) mandates that stock exchanges and registered intermediaries must not maintain any accounts in the name of individuals or entities on the UNSC sanctions list.
- **Delisting Procedures**: Any delisting requests received by banks, stock exchanges, depositories, or SEBI-regulated intermediaries must be electronically forwarded to the Joint Secretary (CTCR), Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
- **Direct Delisting Option**: Individuals or entities seeking removal can also submit requests directly to the Focal Point for Delisting established under resolution 1730 (2006) or through their State of residence/nationality.

## Regulatory Changes

- **Sanctions List Update**: Amendment of 05 entries in UNSC's 1988 (2011) Taliban Sanctions List via Note SC/16425 dated July 30, 2026.
- **Governing Framework**: Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 (Section 51A) and Chapter VII of the United Nations Charter.
- **SEBI Guidelines Alignment**: Governed in accordance with Clause 54 of the SEBI Master Circular on Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Standards and Combating the Financing of Terrorism (CFT) dated June 06, 2024.

## Compliance Requirements

- **Database Screening**: Intermediaries must screen their customer and account databases against the updated UNSC Taliban Sanctions List to verify that no accounts are held under the names of the amended/sanctioned entries.
- **Account Prevention**: Intermediaries must strictly prevent any transaction or account opening for the listed individuals or entities.
- **Request Forwarding**: Promptly forward any delisting request received from customers electronically to the Joint Secretary (CTCR), Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
- **Vigilance and Enforcement**: Maintain active surveillance over client lists and adhere strictly to AML/CFT guidelines to prevent unauthorized accounts.

## Important Dates

- **UNSC Note SC/16425 Date**: July 30, 2026
- **SEBI Communication Date**: August 05, 2026
- **NSE Circular Date**: August 07, 2026
- **Effective Date**: Immediate compliance required upon issuance.

## Impact Assessment

- **Market Impact**: Low. The circular targets individuals and entities on the UNSC Taliban Sanctions List, having no intersection with regular domestic trading or market activity.
- **Operational Impact**: Low to Medium. Operational compliance teams at stock brokerages, depositories, and KRAs must update their screening records. Since screening is generally automated, the manual effort involved is low.
- **Regulatory Risk**: High. Failure to freeze accounts or prevent transactions for sanctioned entities is a direct violation of the UAPA, 1967 and SEBI AML regulations, carrying severe penalties and potential loss of license.
