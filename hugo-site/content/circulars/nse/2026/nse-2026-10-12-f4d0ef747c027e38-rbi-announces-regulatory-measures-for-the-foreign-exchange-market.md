## Summary

The National Stock Exchange has issued a circular highlighting the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) new regulatory measures for the foreign exchange market, promulgated via A.P. (DIR Series) Circular Nos. 25 and 26 on October 10, 2026. These measures tighten market discipline, restrict speculative or repetitive rebooking of derivatives, and introduce strict cash reserve requirements.

## Key Points

- Complete restriction on rebooking cancelled foreign exchange derivative contracts involving INR.
- Threshold for undertaking foreign exchange derivative and exchange-traded currency derivative transactions without establishing underlying exposure reduced from USD 100 million to USD 5 million equivalent.
- Mandate for additional documentation and undertakings confirming that underlying exposures have not been hedged elsewhere.
- Introduction of a 20% Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) in cash with RBI for current account hedging transactions exceeding USD 2 million equivalent.

## Regulatory Changes

- **Rebooking Ban:** Authorised Dealers are prohibited from permitting users to rebook cancelled foreign exchange derivative contracts (deliverable or non-deliverable) involving INR. Rollovers on maturity are still permitted subject to extant regulations.
- **Lowered Unhedged Threshold:** The limit for OTC and exchange-traded currency derivatives without proving underlying exposure drops from USD 100 million to USD 5 million equivalent.
- **FERR Cash Reserve:** Transactions exceeding USD 2 million equivalent involving current account hedging (purchasing foreign currency against INR) require maintaining a 20% FERR cash reserve with RBI.

## Compliance Requirements

- Authorised Dealers and market participants must immediately enforce the USD 5 million ceiling on unhedged derivative positions.
- Implement systems to track and prevent rebooking of cancelled contracts.
- Collect and retain formal undertakings verifying unique underlying exposures.
- Maintain the required 20% cash FERR with RBI for qualifying derivative notional amounts.

## Important Dates

- Effective date aligns with RBI notification dated October 10, 2026, disseminated via NSE circular dated October 12, 2026.

## Impact Assessment

- The measures substantially increase hedging friction and compliance costs for corporates and market participants, while bolstering stability and risk containment in the foreign exchange market.
