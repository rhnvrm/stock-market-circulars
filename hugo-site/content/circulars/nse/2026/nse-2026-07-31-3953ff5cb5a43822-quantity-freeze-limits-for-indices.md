## Summary

The National Stock Exchange of India Limited (NSE) has issued a circular notifying trading members of updated quantity freeze limits for index derivative contracts. This update is implemented in accordance with the computation methodology outlined in Chapter 1.9 ("Quantity freeze for futures & options contracts") of the F&O consolidated circular (NSE/FAOP/73928) dated April 28, 2026. The revised limits will come into effect from August 03, 2026.

## Key Points

- **Updated Quantity Freeze Limits by Index Symbol**:
  - **BANKNIFTY**: 600
  - **NIFTY**: 1800
  - **FINNIFTY**: 1800
  - **MIDCPNIFTY**: 2800
  - **NIFTYNXT50**: 600
- **Contract File Availability**: Members are advised to load the updated `contract.gz` and `NSE_FO_contract_ddmmyyyy.csv.gz` files into their trading systems before trading commences on the effective date. These files can be retrieved via the Extranet directory (`faoftp/faocommon`) or accessed directly on the NSE website.

## Regulatory Changes

- The limits are calculated based on the existing formula specified under Chapter 1.9 of the F&O consolidated circular (Ref: NSE/FAOP/73928) dated April 28, 2026. There are no amendments to the regulatory guidelines or calculation methodology themselves.

## Compliance Requirements

- Trading members must ensure that their front-end trading systems and risk management setups are updated with the revised quantity freeze limits before market hours on August 03, 2026.
- Members should proactively download and apply the latest index contract files from the specified Extranet paths or NSE website reports directory.

## Important Dates

- **Circular Date**: July 31, 2026
- **Effective Date**: August 03, 2026

## Impact Assessment

- **Operational Impact**: Low. Requires a standard routine update of trading system contract configuration files prior to the effective trading session to avoid order execution failures.
- **Market Impact**: Minimal. These limits represent operational risk controls to prevent fat-finger errors or destabilizing single-order flows without restricting standard derivative trading activities.
