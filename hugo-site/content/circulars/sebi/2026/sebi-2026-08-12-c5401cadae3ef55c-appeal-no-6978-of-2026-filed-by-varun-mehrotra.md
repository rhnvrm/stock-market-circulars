## Summary

The SEBI First Appellate Authority (FAA) issued an order on the RTI appeal (Appeal No. 6978 of 2026) filed by Varun Mehrotra against the CPIO of SEBI. The appellant sought copies of corrigendums, addendums, and updates to the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) of Hexaware Technologies Limited (dated February 5, 2025) concerning litigation, civil litigation, employment disputes, EEOC proceedings, or arbitration, as well as copies of communications received by SEBI from the company regarding these disclosures. The CPIO had denied the first query on the grounds that corrigendums/addendums are already in the public domain on the SEBI website, and denied the second query as being exempt under Section 8(1)(e) (fiduciary capacity) of the RTI Act. The FAA upheld the CPIO's decisions, citing established judicial precedents regarding public domain information and fiduciary relationships.

## Key Points

- **Application and Appeal Details:** The appellant filed an RTI application on May 19, 2026. The CPIO responded on June 16, 2026. The appellant subsequently filed an appeal on July 15, 2026.
- **Query 1 (RHP Updates/Corrigendums):** The appellant requested copies of any corrigendum, addendum, revised filing, supplemental disclosure, or clarification submitted by Hexaware Technologies Limited after February 5, 2025 regarding litigation and employment disputes referenced in its RHP.
- **Public Domain Principle Upheld:** The CPIO and the FAA noted that all corrigendums, addendums, or updated filings submitted to SEBI by an issuer company are available in the public domain on the SEBI website. Relying on Delhi High Court and CIC precedents (*Registrar of Companies vs. Dharmendra Kumar Garg* and *Shri K Lall vs. Shri M K Bagri*), the FAA ruled that public authorities are under no obligation to provide information that is already in the public domain.
- **Query 2 (Fiduciary Communications):** The appellant requested copies of any communication from Hexaware Technologies Limited to SEBI after February 5, 2025 seeking to update or correct litigation-related disclosures in its RHP.
- **Fiduciary Exemption Applied:** The CPIO and the FAA held that communications received by SEBI from an issuer company are held in a fiduciary capacity, thereby exempt from disclosure under Section 8(1)(e) of the RTI Act, 2005.

## Regulatory Changes

There are no new regulatory changes introduced by this order. It reinforces existing legal positions regarding the non-disclosure of information available in the public domain and the protection of confidential communications received by SEBI in its fiduciary capacity from issuer companies under the RTI Act, 2005.

## Compliance Requirements

No compliance requirements are imposed on market participants or intermediaries by this individual RTI order. The order serves as a reference for RTI applicants and public information officers regarding the scope of information access, specifically confirming that:
1. Publicly accessible filings, corrigendums, or addendums on the SEBI website need not be physically provided under RTI.
2. Direct corporate communications to SEBI regarding ongoing filings or prospectus amendments are treated as fiduciary and are protected from public disclosure under Section 8(1)(e) of the RTI Act.

## Important Dates

- **May 19, 2026:** Date of the original RTI application filed by the appellant.
- **June 16, 2026:** Date of the CPIO's response letter to the application.
- **July 15, 2026:** Date of the appeal filed by the appellant (Reg. No. SEBIH/A/E/26/00257).
- **August 12, 2026:** Estimated date of the First Appellate Authority's order.

## Impact Assessment

- **Market Impact:** None. This is a standard individual administrative appeal order under the RTI Act, 2005.
- **Operational/Corporate Impact:** Reassures corporate issuers that non-public, preparatory, or clarification-based communications sent to SEBI during prospectus review or subsequent to filings remain confidential and protected under Section 8(1)(e) fiduciary exceptions, even if the final outcome (corrigendum/addendum) is published in the public domain. This protects sensitive corporate correspondences and encourages honest disclosures to the regulator.
