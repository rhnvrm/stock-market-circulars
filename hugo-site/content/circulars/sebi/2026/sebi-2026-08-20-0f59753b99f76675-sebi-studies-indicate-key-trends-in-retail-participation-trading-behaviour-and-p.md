## Summary

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) released two analytical studies conducted by its Department of Economic and Policy Analysis (DEPA) examining retail participation, trading behaviour, and profitability in the Equity Derivatives Segment (EDS) for FY25–FY26. The findings indicate a moderation in retail participation alongside persistently high loss rates among individual traders, particularly in options contracts and near-expiry trading.

## Key Points

- **Decline in Participation:** Active individual traders declined by ~20% from 98.1 lakh in FY25 to 78.6 lakh in FY26; new entrants fell by ~40%.
- **Aggregate Losses:** Aggregate net losses of individual traders reduced to ~₹91,685 crore in FY26 compared to ~₹1.12 lakh crore in FY25, though 87.7% of individual traders still incurred losses.
- **Options Dominance:** Around 92% of aggregate losses incurred by individuals arose from options trading.
- **Profitability by Entity Type:** Proprietary traders recorded the highest gross trading profit at ~₹44,000 crore, followed by FPIs (~₹14,000 crore), Corporates (~₹8,000 crore), and Mutual Funds/Partnership Firms (~₹3,000 crore each). 99% of profits for FPIs and Proprietary traders were made by algo entities.
- **Transaction Costs:** Individual traders incurred transaction costs of ~₹25,000 crore in FY26, remaining broadly unchanged despite lower turnover due to increased Securities Transaction Tax (STT).
- **High Concentration in Near-Expiry Contracts:** Approximately 59% of index options turnover occurred in same-day expiry contracts (0DTE), and 97% within one week of expiry.
- **Portfolio Size vs. Losses:** About 35% of individual EDS traders had no equity holdings, and ~78% had equity portfolios below ₹1 lakh. Small-portfolio traders accounted for ~70% of aggregate losses.

## Regulatory Changes

- No direct regulatory amendments or rule changes introduced in this press release; these are analytical research studies published by SEBI's DEPA.

## Compliance Requirements

- None specified for market participants as these are analytical studies.

## Impact Assessment

The findings highlight ongoing systemic retail losses in the equity derivatives market, reinforcing regulatory scrutiny and potential future policy measures regarding risk disclosures, trading leverage, and investor protection in derivatives segments.
