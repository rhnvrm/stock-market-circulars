## Summary

SEBI has issued a circular permitting Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) to submit a Power of Attorney (PoA) digitally signed in accordance with the provisions of the Information Technology Act, 2000. This measure eliminates the requirement for notarisation, apostillisation, or consularisation, thereby reducing onboarding time and enhancing ease of doing business.

## Key Points

- Permits digitally signed Power of Attorney by FPIs under the IT Act, 2000.
- Eliminates the need for physical notarisation, apostillisation, or consularisation of PoAs given by FPIs to Custodians.
- Modifies Para 9 (B)(iv) of Part B of the FPI Master Circular dated May 30, 2024.

## Regulatory Changes

Para 9(B)(iv) of Part B of the FPI Master Circular is modified to include Power of Attorney executed using digital signatures in accordance with the Information Technology Act, 2000, alongside the existing provisions for notarised/apostilled PoAs.

## Compliance Requirements

Designated Depository Participants (DDPs), Custodians, and FPIs can accept and process digitally signed Power of Attorneys in compliance with the IT Act, 2000, for FPI registration and onboarding.

## Important Dates

The provisions of this circular come into force with effect from August 20, 2026.

## Impact Assessment

Significantly reduces the onboarding timeline and administrative burden for FPI applicants by replacing manual, paper-based apostillisation processes with valid digital signatures.
