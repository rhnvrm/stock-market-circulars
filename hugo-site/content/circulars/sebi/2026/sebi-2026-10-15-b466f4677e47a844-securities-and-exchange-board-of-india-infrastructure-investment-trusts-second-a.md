## Summary

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has notified the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026. These regulations introduce provisions allowing depository receipts to be issued on Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT) units and revise unitholder approval thresholds and voting calculation methodologies, specifically regarding related-party vote exclusions and sponsor exits.

## Key Points

- **Depository Receipts on InvIT Units:** Regulation 14 is amended to permit the issuance of depository receipts on InvIT units, subject to compliance with the board's specified guidelines.
- **Unitholder Approval Thresholds:** Regulation 22 is updated to mandate that resolutions require approval of at least seventy-five percent of the votes cast by unitholders.
- **Exclusion of Related Parties:** When counting votes for resolutions, votes cast by related parties or parties associated with the transaction are explicitly excluded.
- **Sponsor Exit Provisions:** New provisions address scenarios where an InvIT has multiple sponsors and one of the sponsors seeks an exit.

## Regulatory Changes

- Insertion of sub-regulation (5ग) under Regulation 14 permitting depository receipts on InvIT units.
- Amendments to Regulation 22 (sub-regulations 5क, 5ग, and 7) establishing a clear 75% vote threshold in favor and mandating the exclusion of related-party votes during counting.

## Compliance Requirements

- InvIT managers and trustees must align voting procedures and resolution frameworks with the updated 75% approval threshold and related-party exclusion mandates.
- Issuance of depository receipts must strictly follow SEBI's prescribed regulatory framework.

## Important Dates

- **Effective Date:** These regulations come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette (October 2026).

## Impact Assessment

- Facilitates wider global capital participation in Indian InvITs through depository receipts.
- Strengthens corporate governance and minority unitholder protection by refining voting majorities and neutralizing related-party influence in key decisions.
