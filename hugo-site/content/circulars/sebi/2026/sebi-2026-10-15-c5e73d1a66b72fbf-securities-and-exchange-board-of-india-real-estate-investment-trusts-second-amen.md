## Summary

SEBI has issued the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Real Estate Investment Trusts) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026, amending the REIT Regulations, 2014. The amendments introduce provisions enabling the issuance of depository receipts on REIT units, strengthen governance by mandating a 75% unitholder approval threshold excluding related parties for key actions, and outline exit mechanisms for sponsors.

## Key Points

- **Depository Receipts:** Units of REITs can now have depository receipts issued against them, adhering to conditions and guidelines specified by SEBI.
- **Voting & Approval Thresholds:** Revisions to unitholder approval requirements requiring at least 75% of votes cast in favor for relevant resolutions, excluding votes of related/interested parties.
- **Sponsor Exit Mechanism:** In REITs with multiple sponsors, if a sponsor exits, the exiting sponsor or their group (or the continuing sponsor or their group) must provide dissenting unitholders with an exit option by buying out their units as specified by SEBI.

## Regulatory Changes

- **Regulation 2(1)(yu):** Omission of specific text regarding composite real estate projects, industrial parks, and SEZs.
- **Regulation 14(12A):** Insertion of provision permitting the issuance of depository receipts on REIT units.
- **Regulation 18(5)(t):** Clause (t) omitted.
- **Regulation 22(6A) & (8):** Updated voting thresholds (75% majority excluding related parties) for fundamental changes and unitholder approvals, along with provisions for sponsor exits and buyer-funded exit options for dissenting unitholders.

## Compliance Requirements

- REIT managers, trustees, and sponsors must comply with the revised 75% unitholder approval thresholds excluding related parties for applicable corporate actions and alterations.
- Sponsors undertaking exits must provide necessary buyout mechanisms for dissenting unitholders.

## Important Dates

- **Effective Date:** October 14, 2026 (Date of publication in the Official Gazette of India).

## Impact Assessment

These amendments broaden global capital access through depository receipts, enhance minority unitholder protection through stringent voting exclusions for related parties, and clarify operational procedures during sponsor exits.
